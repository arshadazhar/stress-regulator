Pressure, Stress & Dominant Response

Understand your stress patterns and break automatic responses.

The Three Concepts
🎯 Pressure
The subjective experience of being squeezed between conflicting demands. Time pressure, expectations, competing goals, lack of control. Pressure is not stress. You can feel pressure and handle it. Pressure becomes stress when it exceeds your perceived capacity to cope.
⚡ Stress
Your body's nonspecific response to a demand or threat. When pressure activates your nervous system—cortisol rises, adrenaline spikes, heart rate increases, vision narrows. Acute stress can sharpen focus. Chronic stress damages health: weakened immune system, sleep disruption, anxiety, depression.
🔄 Dominant Response
Your most automatic, habitual reaction under stress. The Yerkes-Dodson Law: as stress rises, your brain narrows. You default to what you know best—usually Fight, Flight, Freeze, or Fawn. Your dominant response was adaptive once (it kept you safe). Now it often makes things worse.

⚡ When Pressure Builds (Before Stress Activates)
The window to intervene. Early awareness is power. Notice the pressure before your nervous system floods.

Tools: Breathing, boundary-setting, asking for help, breaking tasks down, saying no.
🔥 When Stress is Active (During the Reaction)
Your brain is narrowed. You can't access wisdom. You need somatic tools—tools for your body, not your mind.

Tools: Cold water on face, movement, paced breathing, muscle relaxation, grounding (5-4-3-2-1).
🔄 Breaking the Dominant Response Loop
Your automatic response made sense once. Now it makes things worse. Breaking it requires:

1. Awareness: Notice the pattern (that's happening now)
2. Pause: Create space between stimulus and response
3. Choice: Practice a new response (even small differences help)
4. Repetition: New patterns build through practice
